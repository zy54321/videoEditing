# Editing & Rhythm — Common Craft

## Decision Priority (when options conflict)

```
Emotion
  → Story
    → Rhythm
      → Eye Trace / Attention
        → Spatial / Technical Continuity
```

Safety and factual integrity sit **above** this stack.

---

## Cut Motivation

Every cut should have a reason. Common motivations:

- **Action cut** — movement continues across edit
- **Reaction cut** — show response to prior action
- **Contrast cut** — juxtapose states or ideas
- **Montage** — compress time or ideas
- **J-cut / L-cut** — audio leads or trails picture for flow

Also: semantic turn, emphasis, subject change, breath reset.

Agents need **not** explain every ordinary cut to Owner.

A narration sentence or a Narrative Beat change is not by itself a picture cut. Ordinary semantic turns can stay inside a shot that is already showing the action.

---

## Continuous Shot Preservation

When one local passage is the same source, keep it as one clip unless a cut has a purpose.

Preserve the shot when all of the following hold:

- The finished timeline positions abut. A gap or an overlap, including one frame, stays split
- Resolve source frames abut exactly, and the joined clip requests the same source frames as the two clips together
- Playback speed matches
- Crop, zoom, and transform match, or neither side sets them
- No stated Jump Cut, editorial exemption, transition, or special effect asks for a cut

Emotion, contrast, a real action change, and an intentional rhythmic cut stay split. A Jump Cut needs an explicit editorial purpose. A later sentence of narration can play over the same continuous picture.

Do not close a source gap by extending the clip across frames that were not in either trim. One skipped or repeated source frame is enough to keep the shots split. A hole is a planning problem: choose one continuous in/out later, or leave the shots separate. Suggesting that replan does not itself rewrite the in/out.

A long hold on one continuous action is not the same fault as showing the same asset again after a real gap. Do not treat every long take as a reason to swap in a different asset.

---

## Pacing & Rhythm

Avoid simplistic rules like “cut every 3 seconds.”

Consider:

- information density
- emotional temperature
- narration cadence
- visual motion energy
- intentional **hold** (let a moment land)
- **silence / breath** sections
- **original-sound** passages

---

## Repetition

Repetition can:

- reinforce a theme
- create rhythm
- bore the audience if unmotivated

Reuse requires changed range, framing, or narrative role.

---

## Motion Cadence

- Source fps ≠ natural motion speed
- Timeline fps mismatch can cause judder
- Speed change alters rhythm — verify after retime

Do not assume interpolation without verified capability.

---

## Transition Motivation

Transitions express relationship:

- hard cut — continuity or contrast
- dissolve — time / place soft change
- effect transition — only when motivated

“More professional” is not motivation.
